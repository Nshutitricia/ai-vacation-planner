# Destination Notes: Reykjavik and Iceland

Iceland's weather changes quickly, and a sunny morning can turn into wind and rain by afternoon. Layered clothing and a waterproof outer shell are more useful than a single heavy coat, regardless of season.

Renting a car is the most practical way to see sights outside Reykjavik, since public transport to places like the Golden Circle or the south coast waterfalls is limited and often slower than driving directly.

In winter, daylight hours are very short, sometimes only four to five hours around the darkest weeks of December, which limits how much sightseeing is realistic in a single day. Summer, by contrast, has nearly continuous daylight, which some travelers find disorienting for sleep.

The Northern Lights are never guaranteed and depend on clear skies and solar activity, not just the time of year. Traveling with flexible plans and checking an aurora forecast app improves the odds significantly compared to a single fixed viewing night.

Reykjavik itself is a small, walkable city, and most visitors do not need a car for the days spent purely within the capital. The Blue Lagoon is popular but often requires advance booking, sometimes weeks ahead during peak season, since walk-in capacity is limited.

Iceland is expensive relative to many European destinations, particularly for dining out. Grocery store meals and preparing some food in accommodations with kitchens is a common way travelers manage costs over longer stays.
