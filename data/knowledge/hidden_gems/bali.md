# Hidden Gems in Bali

While Kuta and Seminyak draw the largest crowds, the village of Sidemen in eastern Bali offers rice terrace views comparable to the more famous Tegalalang terraces near Ubud, without the crowds or the entrance fees charged at the popular tourist spots.

Munduk, in the mountainous north of the island, is home to several waterfalls that receive far fewer visitors than the well-known Tegenungan waterfall closer to the south. The cooler mountain climate is also a welcome break from the coastal heat.

Amed, on the northeast coast, is a quiet fishing village with black sand beaches and calm water suitable for beginner snorkeling and diving, in contrast to the busier dive scene around Nusa Penida. A sunken Japanese shipwreck near Amed is accessible even to shallow snorkelers.

The Banyu Wana Amertha waterfall near Tabanan requires a short trek through rice fields and is rarely crowded compared to waterfalls closer to Ubud, making it a good stop for travelers who want a genuine local scene rather than a photo queue.

Small warungs, family-run food stalls serving Balinese home cooking, are usually far better value and more authentic than restaurants aimed at tourists in the main resort areas. Asking a local guesthouse owner for their personal recommendation often leads to better meals than searching review sites.

Lesser-known temples like Pura Luhur Batukau, on the slopes of Bali's second highest volcano, offer a much quieter alternative to Tanah Lot for travelers who want to experience a working temple without large tour groups.
