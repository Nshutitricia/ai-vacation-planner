# Local Tips for Rome

Many restaurants near major tourist sites like the Trevi Fountain or the Spanish Steps charge tourist prices for average food. Walking two or three streets away from these landmarks usually finds better and cheaper trattorias used by locals rather than visitors.

Tap water in Rome is safe to drink, and the small metal drinking fountains scattered around the city, called nasoni, provide free cold water. Bringing a refillable bottle saves money and avoids unnecessary plastic waste in the summer heat.

The Colosseum and Roman Forum share a combined ticket, and buying it at the less crowded Forum entrance rather than the Colosseum entrance often means a much shorter wait, especially in peak season.

Many churches in Rome, including some containing significant art like Caravaggio paintings, request modest dress: covered shoulders and knees. Carrying a light scarf is an easy way to comply without changing an entire outfit.

Lunch in Rome is typically a lighter, faster affair than dinner, and many small delis and pizza-by-the-slice shops cater specifically to a quick midday meal, which is often better value than sitting down at a full restaurant.

August is when many Romans themselves go on holiday, and some smaller, family-run restaurants close for one or two weeks entirely. It is worth checking opening status in advance if visiting during this month, since this can catch tourists off guard.

Trastevere, on the west bank of the Tiber, has a livelier evening scene than the historic center, with narrow cobbled streets full of small bars and family-run restaurants popular with locals rather than tourists.
