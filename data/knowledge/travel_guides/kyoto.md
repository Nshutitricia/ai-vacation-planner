# Kyoto Travel Guide

Kyoto has more than a thousand temples and shrines, so choosing a small number to visit properly matters more than trying to see them all. Fushimi Inari, with its thousands of orange torii gates, is at its most peaceful very early in the morning, well before the tour buses arrive around mid-morning.

Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, is stunning but often crowded, and visits are typically short since the temple grounds follow a fixed walking path with limited stopping points. Arashiyama's bamboo grove nearby is free to walk through and is best experienced at dawn for both light and quiet.

Traditional ryokan accommodations often include a kaiseki dinner, a multi-course meal built around seasonal ingredients. Guests are usually expected to arrive by early evening to enjoy the full experience, since check-in times can be earlier than at typical hotels.

Spring cherry blossom season, usually late March to early April, and autumn foliage in November are the two busiest and most beautiful times to visit. Hotel prices rise significantly during these windows, and popular sites can be very crowded.

Kyoto's public buses can be confusing for first-time visitors because routes overlap and some require exact change. A one-day bus pass is often worth buying if visiting more than two or three temple sites in a single day, since fares add up quickly otherwise.

The Gion district is a working neighborhood, not a theme park, and visitors are asked not to photograph geiko or maiko without permission, particularly in narrow alleyways where crowding has become a real problem for residents.
