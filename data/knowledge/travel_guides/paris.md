# Paris Travel Guide

Paris rewards travelers who mix its famous landmarks with quieter neighborhood exploration. The Eiffel Tower is most peaceful at opening time, around 9am, before the tour groups arrive. Buying tickets online in advance avoids the longest queues, which can stretch over an hour in peak summer months.

The Louvre is enormous, and trying to see everything in one visit usually leaves people exhausted and unsatisfied. A better approach is to pick two or three wings in advance — the Denon wing for the Mona Lisa and Winged Victory, or the Richelieu wing for Northern European painting — rather than wandering without a plan.

Montmartre is best visited in the early morning or early evening, when the crowds around Sacre-Coeur thin out and the light on the basilica's white stone is at its best. The steep streets nearby still have a village feel, with small art studios and cafes tucked between the tourist shops.

For food, Parisians eat dinner later than many visitors expect, typically after 7:30pm, and restaurants that look empty at 6:30 are not necessarily bad — they simply haven't opened for service yet. Markets like Marche des Enfants Rouges are a good way to eat well without a formal restaurant sitting.

The Seine river cruises are popular but can be crowded midday. An evening cruise after sunset, when the bridges and monuments are lit, is generally considered more memorable and less rushed.

Public transport is efficient and the Metro covers almost the entire city. A carnet of ten tickets is usually more economical than single fares for a multi-day stay, and walking between nearby attractions is often faster than taking the Metro for very short distances.
